    About site counts, SA Score and source labels

    c counts reactive connection sites, not point-group symmetry. SA Score runs from 1 easier to 10 harder: it is a molecular heuristic, not a synthesis route or COF yield prediction. Reference-only entries can be inspected and edited but cannot be used in automatic construction. Source-catalog labels remain qualified by the review evidence in each monomer's details.

    Drawing shortcuts

    Click an atom to extend a bond. Drag to connect, or hold Alt for a free angle. Select a ring tool and click an outside bond to fuse a ring.

    Hover over a bond and press 1, 2, 3 or D to change it. Over an atom, type an element key or + / − to change charge. Delete removes the highlighted item. Ctrl+Shift+K cleans the layout.

    Periodic table

    Common elements for molecular and coordination drawings. Pick an element, then click the canvas or an existing atom.

    A local chemical drawing kit

    1. Choose your monomers. Use a preset, paste SMILES, or draw atoms and bonds. The local chemistry engine checks each structure.
    2. Build a finite patch. Choose linkage chemistry, check the reactive groups, and select a compatible connectivity template. Inspect attachment ports; the preset is a design choice.
    3. Choose a style and save. Chemical, layered, and schematic styles have separate depiction scopes. PNG and SVG preserve the drawing. MOL preserves the atom/bond graph. A project JSON preserves monomers, recipe, coordinates, and style choices.

    Source recipes come from provisional agent annotations. Their source hash and qualified COFex record are separate from a designed variant. A finite patch does not specify a full periodic crystal, and COF graph extraction accuracy has not been established by this drawing tool.

    The basic editor supports element, charge, single/double/triple bond, and ring editing. It does not assign new wedge or axial stereochemistry. Model inference is not required to draw.

    Composition map

    How to read the map

    One point groups records with the same precursor graphs and indexed linkage. Nearby points share local chemical features. Global distances and axis directions have no physical units. Use the zoom buttons, drag with a mouse to pan, or search to inspect overlapping points. Coordinates use t-SNE on mean radius-2 Morgan fingerprints and the linkage class, after PCA (48 components, perplexity 30, seed 42).

    Crystal model properties

    Geometric screening method

    Each point represents one periodic CIF. COFdraw uses a 1.82 Å probe, a requested 0.9 Å grid and 16 surface samples per atom. LCD and PLD are lower estimates. Surface and connected pore volume depend on the probe and grid resolution. Hypothetical cells assume aligned AA stacking and have passed periodic-bond and steric checks. Adsorption and electronic targets require separate calculations.

    Methods and pending targets
    Composition assumes complete reaction at the selected sites and a dry repeat with the smallest balanced block ratio. A bounded sample of unreported planar pairs was selected across topology and repeat-mass groups. Periodic properties use the same geometric protocol as the crystal plot. Batch ZIPs include candidate IDs, precursor SMILES, status columns and available CIFs. Band gap and pure CO₂ and CH₄ uptake at 298 K and 1 bar are initialized to 0 with status “pending”. Pending values are excluded from rankings and distributions.
    For comparison, published COF screening combines crystal geometry with separately simulated adsorption targets. The COF Space, ACS Materials Letters

      Pore geometry
      Deterministic pore geometry from a periodic CIF or model: PLD, LCD, connected accessible area and volume, density, and void fraction.
      Work limits may coarsen the grid. Results show the effective resolution and diameter bounds.
