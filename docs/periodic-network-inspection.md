# Periodic connectivity inspection

`cofdraw_network.inspect_periodic_network(structure, bond_scale=1.15, tolerance_angstrom=0.15)` inspects an explicit periodic atomic structure. It accepts the same full-cell dictionary as `normalize_structure`, including structures returned by CIF import. Fractional coordinates are wrapped, explicit hydrogens participate, and no atoms are added. Results are JSON-safe and record units, parameters, radii, RDKit version, and a structure hash.

The default candidate bond rule is `distance <= 1.15 * (r_cov[a] + r_cov[b]) + 0.15 Å`. The adjustable ranges are 0.8–1.4 for the scale and 0–0.4 Å for the additive tolerance. The radius values come from [RDKit's periodic table](https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.Chem.PeriodicTable.html). This is a geometric heuristic, not formal bond-order perception. Radius-based neighbor cutoffs are also used by [ASE's neighbor-list tools](https://docs.ase-lib.org/ase/neighborlist.html); the particular defaults here are COFdraw screening choices, not universal chemical constants.

Each returned edge contains zero-based atom indices `a`, `b`, an integer cell `translation`, `distance_angstrom`, `radius_sum_angstrom`, and `cutoff_angstrom`. Cell vectors are rows. Its Cartesian displacement is `(fractional[b] + translation - fractional[a]) @ cell`. The reversed edge with opposite translation is equivalent and is not duplicated. An atom's nonzero self-image edges are retained, and each contributes two neighbors to its coordination count.

The image search uses the reciprocal-cell bound `|delta[k] + translation[k]| <= R * ||inverse(cell)[:, k]||`, together with the fractional-coordinate spread. This includes every image inside the largest search radius, even for skew cells and bonds extending beyond the nearest 27 translated cells. It does not assume that independently rounding fractional coordinates gives the closest image. Limits are 2,000 supplied sites, 1,331 translated cells, 120 million directed image-pair candidates, and 30,000 stored short-range contacts. Excessive work fails explicitly; the graph is never silently truncated. Reduce the cell or inspect a smaller primitive representation if necessary.

The finite quotient graph is traversed with integer translation offsets. Non-tree edges give cycle translations; their exact integer rank is the inferred component dimension: 0 for finite objects, 1 for chains, 2 for layers, and 3 for a spanning network. This follows the quotient-graph dimensionality principle described by [Gao et al., npj Computational Materials 6, 143 (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-020-00409-0). A finite molecule crossing a cell boundary still has dimension 0. A component's returned independent cycle vectors span its translation directions; they are not claimed to form a primitive integer lattice basis.

`components` are connected components of the **quotient graph**. They are not a count of all connected objects in the infinite crystal: translated copies and equivalent disconnected interpenetrating networks require additional analysis. No RCSR net name, interpenetration multiplicity, space group, COF identity, stacking assignment, or experimentally validated bonding is produced. Atomic coordination is the number of inferred neighbors, not valence; bonds to metal or ionic sites need specialist interpretation.

Useful diagnostics include:

- Per-atom coordination, element-wise coordination histograms, isolated sites, and loose upper bounds for common organic atoms.
- Implausibly short contacts below `max(0.35 Å, 0.65 * radius_sum)`. These are reported and excluded from the inferred graph; their presence makes dimension interpretation unreliable until the geometry is checked.
- Contacts within 0.10 Å of the cutoff and component/dimension results with the cutoff shifted by ±0.10 Å. Changes flag threshold sensitivity; absence of a change is not a confidence interval or proof of correct chemistry.
- The minimum distance among candidates inside the global radius-based search envelope. `minimum_candidate_distance_angstrom` is `null` if no pair enters that envelope; it is not an unconditional global nearest-neighbor search.

The bundled hydrogen-complete templates recover 2D connectivity for the two HCB AA models, two 2D quotient components for the HCB AB model, and 3D connectivity for the DIA model. None has isolated sites, short-contact warnings, or a changed classification under the ±0.10 Å check. Tests additionally cover finite molecules spanning a boundary, periodic self edges, skew cells requiring translation `(2,-1,0)`, wide brute-force image comparison, exact integer rank, basis changes, supercell invariance, abnormal H coordination, and rejected resource bounds. These validate the implementation on the stated fixtures; they do not experimentally validate the model structures.
